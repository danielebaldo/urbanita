About Urbanìta

From the English dictionary, an urbanite is nothing but a city-dweller. Etymologically from the Latin urbanus, an urbanite is literally “a person of the city.”

This noun can actually hide multiple meanings, both on the anthropological and societal levels. Philosophically, an urbanite exists and coexists with and within the city, as there is no urbanite without the urban. In this sense, the urbanite is a state of mind and of consciousness. A person of and by the city, observing, contemplating, admiring the urban environment and its multifaceted dimensions.

Urbanìta is an Italian translation of urbanite — a deliberate coinage taken from my Stratosferica family, not to be confused with urbanità, which already exists in Italian but means something else entirely: courtesy, refinement, good manners. No dictionary offers a true equivalent of urbanite in this sense, and the usual synonyms — cittadino, abitante della città — fail to capture the deep complexity of the original word.

Urbanìta is my tribute to cities, a digital space to host my thoughts around them and those of others, while building funny things that might evolve into more serious things, or not.

Thank you for reading this.

About this website

Urbanìta is a small city-lookup tool, built as static files that talk to the Wikipedia and Wikidata APIs straight from your browser. Every search resolves an article on Wikipedia, then checks Wikidata to confirm the result is a genuine settlement — a city, town, village, municipality, and so on — before showing it. If it isn’t, Urbanìta says so and offers real alternatives instead of guessing.

Where each part comes from

  • The summary and the photograph are the opening of the city’s Wikipedia article, and every result links back to it.
  • The key facts — population, country, area, elevation, timezone — come from Wikidata, which is also what decides whether a result is really a city. Missing entries are simply left out rather than guessed at.
  • On the map plots the article’s own coordinates over OpenStreetMap-based tiles.
  • In the news is a narrow, curated feed rather than general news about the city: a handful of urbanism and architecture publications, plus a broader news search filtered to the same subjects — which is why travel pieces, and occasionally culture or sport, turn up too. Most cities on most days have nothing, and that is normal. It’s also the one part that doesn’t come straight from your browser: it goes through a small proxy of mine, which keeps an API key private and caches each city’s results by the hour.
  • On film asks Wikidata for films whose narrative or filming location is the city, best-known first. The list is only as complete as Wikidata’s editors have made it, so plenty of films set in a given city won’t be there — and one merely named after a city, without being set in it, won’t either.
